## [af-2023.definition] Clinical definition of AF

A diagnostic episode of atrial fibrillation is defined by a continuous
stretch of AF lasting at least 30 seconds as observed on an
electrocardiographic recording. Shorter runs of irregular atrial
activity do not meet the guideline's diagnostic threshold for AF.

The same 30-second threshold is used by FDA-cleared consumer-grade
rhythm notification features (e.g. Apple Watch Irregular Rhythm
Notification, FDA DEN180042) for alerting a user to possible AF.

---

## [af-2023.ahre-under-5min] Device-detected AHRE under 5 minutes

Atrial high-rate episodes ("AHRE") of less than 5 minutes detected by an
implantable device or wearable, in a patient without another indication
for anticoagulation, **should not** lead to initiation of oral
anticoagulation on the basis of the AHRE alone.

This is the lowest-duration bucket in the guideline's AHRE framework.
Episodes this short are treated as non-actionable from an anticoagulation
standpoint.

---

## [af-2023.ahre-5min-to-24h] Device-detected AHRE of 5 minutes to 24 hours

For patients with a device-detected AHRE lasting between 5 minutes and
24 hours and with a CHA₂DS₂-VASc score of 3 or higher (or equivalent
stroke risk), it **may be reasonable** (Class 2b) to initiate oral
anticoagulation within a shared decision-making framework that considers
the episode duration and the patient's individual risk profile.

Patients in this duration bucket with lower stroke-risk scores do not
automatically meet the criteria for anticoagulation; the recommendation
is conditional on risk stratification.

---

## [af-2023.ahre-24h-plus] Device-detected AHRE of 24 hours or more

For patients with a device-detected AHRE lasting 24 hours or longer and
with a CHA₂DS₂-VASc score of 2 or more (or equivalent stroke risk), it
**is reasonable** (Class 2a) to initiate oral anticoagulation within a
shared decision-making framework that considers the episode duration and
the patient's individual risk profile.

Episodes of 24 hours or longer are treated as carrying stroke-risk
implications comparable to clinical AF.

---

## [af-2023.af-burden] AF burden as stroke-risk modifier

For patients in the intermediate annual-risk band for thromboembolism,
the guideline lists AF burden among the stroke-risk modifiers that can
inform the anticoagulation decision. Higher AF burden, together with
persistent or permanent (vs. paroxysmal) pattern, favours initiating
anticoagulation; lower burden can support deferral.

The guideline does not prescribe a specific numeric burden cut-off for
initiating anticoagulation. References in the supporting literature use
various thresholds (commonly 5.5 hours/day or 10% burden) to stratify
risk, but these are not themselves guideline-level recommendations.

---

## [af-2023.atrial-flutter] Atrial flutter stroke-prevention equivalence

Patients with atrial flutter should be managed with the same approach to
stroke prevention as patients with atrial fibrillation. Anticoagulation
decisions use the same framework and the same CHA₂DS₂-VASc-based risk
stratification; flutter episodes are not considered lower-risk than AF
episodes of comparable duration.

---

## [af-2023.rate-control-targets] Resting ventricular rate targets

For patients with AF requiring rate control, a lenient target of a
resting heart rate below 110 bpm is acceptable (Class 2b) if the patient
remains asymptomatic and left ventricular function is preserved. A
strict target of below 80 bpm at rest may be considered (Class 2b) when
symptoms persist under a lenient strategy or when tachycardia-mediated
cardiomyopathy is a concern.

These targets apply to ongoing management of known AF, not to acute
alerting thresholds in monitoring devices.

---

## [af-2023.rhythm-control-class1] Rhythm-control indication

For patients with AF and reduced left ventricular ejection fraction
together with high AF burden, a trial of rhythm control is a Class 1
indication. For recent-onset or symptomatic AF, rhythm control receives
a Class 2a indication.

This section is informational for advice generation; monitoring devices
do not themselves recommend rhythm-control interventions.
