**Full citation**: Kusumoto FM, Schoenfeld MH, Barrett C, et al.

Clinical Practice Guidelines and the Heart Rhythm Society.
*Circulation*. 2019;140(8):e382–e482.
doi: 10.1161/CIR.0000000000000628

**Source URL**: https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000000628

## [brady-2018.definitions] Bradycardia and sinus bradycardia definitions

The guideline frames its evaluation pathways around bradycardia defined
as a heart rate below the normal resting range. Sinus bradycardia is
conventionally defined as a sinus-origin rhythm with a ventricular rate
of less than 50 beats per minute.

Heart rates below 40 bpm are treated as a cut-off of concern in the
guideline's evaluation algorithms for sinus node dysfunction, though
the guideline does not attach a fixed numeric threshold to mandatory
action.

---

## [brady-2018.symptomatic-principle] Symptomatic-bradycardia principle

The guideline's central principle for intervention is that pacing is
indicated for **symptomatic** bradycardia attributable to sinus node
dysfunction or conduction delay. Asymptomatic bradycardia, even when
the heart rate is low by population norms, is not itself a pacing
indication.

For a consumer monitoring context, this principle translates to:
low heart rate alone does not warrant urgent alerting; clinical context
(symptoms, time of day, activity level) determines whether the finding
is actionable.

---

## [brady-2018.nocturnal-bradycardia] Nocturnal bradycardia

The guideline explicitly notes that nocturnal bradycardia, including
heart rates below 40 bpm during sleep, is common in the general adult
population and is usually asymptomatic. In otherwise healthy individuals
these findings do not require pacing and do not typically warrant
clinical concern in isolation.

For patients with sleep-disordered breathing, nocturnal bradycardia is
more frequent and is often mitigated by treating the underlying sleep
apnea rather than by pacing.

The clinical implication for monitoring systems: a low heart rate
observed during typical sleep hours carries different significance from
the same heart rate observed during waking hours.

---

## [brady-2018.av-block-ventricular-rate] AV block with slow ventricular response

High-grade atrioventricular block associated with a ventricular escape
rate below 40 bpm in an awake, symptomatic patient is a Class 1
indication for permanent pacing. This is among the strongest pacing
indications in the guideline.

A consumer monitoring system cannot distinguish sinus-origin bradycardia
from AV block without additional morphological analysis, so this
recommendation does not directly drive monitoring thresholds, but it
establishes that sustained heart rates below 40 bpm in an awake
patient warrant clinical evaluation.

---

## [brady-2018.evaluation-pathway] Initial evaluation for sinus node dysfunction

The guideline's evaluation pathway for suspected sinus node dysfunction
begins with documentation of symptoms and correlation with bradycardia
on ECG monitoring. Relevant symptoms include syncope, presyncope,
fatigue, and reduced exercise tolerance. The diagnostic workup is
oriented around establishing the symptom-bradycardia correlation, not
around meeting a specific heart-rate threshold.

This reinforces that pure heart-rate thresholds (e.g. "HR below 40
equals alert") are insufficient without context; the guideline-aligned
action is clinical evaluation, not immediate intervention.

---

## [brady-2018.reversible-causes] Reversible causes must be excluded

Before attributing bradycardia to intrinsic sinus node or conduction
system disease, reversible causes should be excluded. These include
medication effects (beta-blockers, calcium channel blockers, digoxin,
antiarrhythmics), hypothyroidism, sleep apnea, and ischemia.

For advice generation, this means that an advice stream recommending
medical evaluation for bradycardia should frame the evaluation as
"identify whether this is caused by something reversible" rather than
"assume pacing is needed."
