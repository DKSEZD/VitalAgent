## [svt-2015.scope] Scope of the SVT guideline

For the purposes of this guideline, supraventricular tachycardia (SVT)
refers to any arrhythmia originating above and including the bundle of
His, and specifically excludes atrial fibrillation. Conditions covered
include atrioventricular nodal reentrant tachycardia (AVNRT),
atrioventricular reentrant tachycardia (AVRT), focal atrial tachycardia,
multifocal atrial tachycardia, junctional tachycardia, atrial flutter,
and inappropriate sinus tachycardia.

---

## [svt-2015.tachycardia-definition] Tachycardia definition and sustained elevation

The guideline notes that in normal individuals the resting sinus rate is
generally 50–90 bpm. Tachycardia is defined as a heart rate exceeding
100 bpm in an adult. Physiological sinus tachycardia (the normal
response to exercise, stress, fever, anemia, hyperthyroidism, etc.) is
not itself an arrhythmia; diagnostic concern arises when tachycardia
occurs at rest without an identifiable physiological cause.

For a monitoring context, the clinically relevant signal is not a
single 100+ bpm reading but sustained elevation. Brief excursions above
100 bpm occur routinely with activity and are not actionable. Sustained
resting tachycardia, by contrast, is the defining feature of
inappropriate sinus tachycardia (see the IST section below) and also
warrants exclusion of reversible causes such as dehydration, anemia, or
thyroid dysfunction.

## [svt-2015.paroxysmal-svt] Paroxysmal SVT characteristics

Paroxysmal SVT is characterised by sudden onset and termination of a
regular, typically narrow-QRS-complex tachycardia. The underlying
mechanisms include AVNRT, orthodromic AVRT using an accessory pathway,
and atrial tachycardia. Onset and termination occur over one or a few
beats, distinguishing paroxysmal SVT from sinus tachycardia (which
accelerates and decelerates gradually).

For monitoring, a sudden change from normal sinus rhythm to a regular
rhythm with a markedly higher ventricular rate is consistent with a
paroxysmal SVT episode.

---

## [svt-2015.avnrt-rate] AVNRT ventricular rate range

AVNRT is the most common form of SVT. The ventricular rate during AVNRT
is typically 180 to 200 beats per minute but ranges from 110 to greater
than 250 bpm, with rare cases below 100 bpm.

This establishes that sustained ventricular rates in the 180+ range, in
the absence of exertion, are within the clinically expected range for
an SVT episode requiring acute evaluation.

---

## [svt-2015.inappropriate-sinus-tachycardia] Inappropriate sinus tachycardia

Inappropriate sinus tachycardia is characterised by a resting heart
rate greater than 100 bpm without an identifiable physiologic cause.
The diagnosis requires excluding conditions that would produce
appropriate sympathetic elevation of heart rate (dehydration, fever,
anemia, hyperthyroidism, pheochromocytoma, medications, postural
orthostatic tachycardia syndrome).

This is a chronic-finding diagnosis, not an acute-alert condition; a
monitoring device observing a persistently elevated resting heart rate
should route to clinical evaluation, not to urgent alerting.

---

## [svt-2015.acute-management-hemodynamic-instability] Hemodynamic instability

For a patient presenting with SVT and hemodynamic instability (hypotension,
altered mental status, ischemic chest pain, signs of shock, acute heart
failure), synchronised cardioversion is a Class 1 indication.

A monitoring device cannot directly detect hemodynamic instability, but
this recommendation anchors the "seek immediate attention" advice for
sustained very-high ventricular rates that the user perceives as
symptomatic.

---

## [svt-2015.acute-management-stable] Acute management of stable SVT

For haemodynamically stable SVT, vagal manoeuvres and intravenous
adenosine are Class 1 acute interventions. If these are ineffective and
the patient remains stable, intravenous beta blockers, diltiazem, or
verapamil may be used (Class 2a).

For advice generation, this context matters when discussing what to
expect at a clinical evaluation, but a consumer monitoring device does
not itself recommend these interventions.

---

## [svt-2015.atrial-flutter] Atrial flutter within the SVT framework

Atrial flutter is included within this guideline's scope as a
supraventricular tachycardia. However, for stroke-prevention purposes
atrial flutter is managed using the atrial fibrillation guideline's
framework (see AF-2023). The SVT guideline covers atrial flutter's
rhythm-control and rate-control considerations, not its anticoagulation
indications.
